## SOUTHERN-CALIFORNIA-ENERGY-CONSUMPTION-DATA-ANALYSIS

### Project Overview

Energy consumption patterns play a critical role in cost management, operational efficiency, and environmental sustainability. Understanding how different factors influence energy usage can help organizations make informed decisions and optimize resource allocation.

This project analyzes energy consumption data from residential, commercial, and industrial buildings in Southern California between 2021 and 2023. The goal is to identify consumption trends, evaluate key drivers of energy demand, and uncover opportunities for improving energy efficiency.

The analysis was conducted using data analytic tools to transform raw energy data into actionable insights that support sustainable energy management.

### Project Objectives

The objectives of this analysis are to:

- Examine energy consumption trends across different building types.
- Identify the major drivers of energy usage.
- Analyze the relationship between occupancy and energy demand.
- Evaluate the impact of building and equipment age on energy consumption.
- Assess carbon emissions associated with energy usage.
- Provide recommendations for improving energy efficiency.

###  Tools Used

### Dataset Description

The dataset contains energy consumption records from residential, commercial, and industrial buildings in Southern California from 2021 to 2023.

### Key Insights

1. Residential buildings consumed the most energy, accounting for over 665,000 kWh—more than commercial and industrial buildings combined.
2. Energy consumption increased during peak occupancy periods, showing a strong link between human activity and electricity demand.
3. Older buildings and equipment recorded higher energy usage, suggesting lower energy efficiency.
4. Higher HVAC and lighting usage significantly increased total energy consumption, making them major drivers of electricity demand.
5. Energy demand varied throughout the day, with peak periods placing greater pressure on the power grid.
6. Buildings with higher energy consumption also generated more carbon emissions, highlighting the environmental impact of inefficient energy use.

### Recommendations

1. Upgrade aging equipment and infrastructure to reduce unnecessary energy consumption and improve efficiency.
2. Implement smart lighting systems such as motion sensors and automated controls to minimize electricity waste.
3. Optimize HVAC operations by scheduling maintenance and adjusting usage during low-occupancy periods.
4. Introduce energy-monitoring dashboards to track consumption patterns and support faster decision-making.
5. Encourage energy-saving practices among occupants, such as switching off unused appliances and equipment.
6. Prioritize energy-efficiency programs in residential buildings, since they account for the largest share of consumption.
7. Adopt renewable energy solutions, such as solar power, to reduce dependence on traditional energy sources.
8. Monitor carbon emissions alongside energy usage to support sustainability goals and environmental planning.
